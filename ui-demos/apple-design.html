What the skill is offering

Eleven principles out of Emil Kowalski's apple-design skill, each one live and touchable. Left is what the app does today. Right is what the principle asks for. Drag, flick and press both — reading the difference is not the same as feeling it. Nothing here is wired into Practice Partner; two of these (§2 and §14) have already been taken, and the rest are here so you can decide with your hands rather than from a list.

§1

Response — answer the press, not the release

The moment lag appears, directness "falls off a cliff". A button that waits for click has already lost by the time it lights up — and on touch, click can trail the finger by a hundred milliseconds or more.

Answers on release

Press and hold. Nothing happens until you let go.
—

Answers on press

Lights the instant your finger lands, and cancels if you slide away.
—
Already right in the app. Sixteen :active rules across the stylesheet — press states fire on pointer-down.
§2

Direct manipulation — respect where they grabbed

Touch and content move together. Snapping the element's centre to the finger on grab breaks the illusion in the first frame — you did not pick it up by the middle.

Snaps to centre

Grab it by a corner and it jumps.
drag

Keeps the offset

Grab it anywhere; that spot stays under your finger.
drag
Just fixed. The reader's pinch had exactly this bug in two dimensions — the music slid out from under your fingers as it grew, up to 1465px of drift on a 1024px screen. It now stays put.
§3

Interruptibility — the most important one

"The thought and the gesture happen in parallel." Every animation must be grabbable and reversible mid-flight. A CSS transition cannot do this: it owns the property until it finishes.

CSS transition

Tap to send it across, then try to grab it. It ignores you, then jumps.
tap

Spring, from the live value

Tap to send it, then catch it mid-flight. It comes with you.
tap
Not in the app. The reader has no travelling animations to interrupt — page turns are instant by design. This matters the day you animate one.
§4

Behaviour over animation — two knobs, not a duration

Apple replaced mass/stiffness/damping with damping ratio (overshoot: 1.0 none, below that it bounces) and response (how fast it gets there, in seconds). Bounce is earned by momentum — a flick deserves it, a tap does not.

Try the two numbers

Tap the stage to send it. Apple ships 1.0 / 0.4 for a reposition, 0.8 / 0.3 for a sheet.
tap
Finding #5 in the review. The app's --spring is cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1) — that 1.56 is overshoot, and it is the default on ~14 transitions including button presses. Set damping to 0.75 above to feel what a tapped button currently does.
§5

Velocity handoff — no seam between drag and animation

When the finger leaves, the animation must continue at the speed the finger was going. Drop that and there is a visible stitch where your hand stops mattering.

Velocity thrown away

Flick it hard. It stalls, then eases home at the same speed however you threw it.
flick

Velocity carried through

Flick it hard and it keeps going. Nudge it gently and it drifts.
flick
§6

Momentum projection — land where the gesture was going

"Take a small input and make a big output." Don't snap from where the finger let go — project where it would have coasted to, then snap to the nearest mark from there. project(v) = (v/1000) · d/(1−d), d ≈ 0.998.

Snaps from the release point

A hard flick and a gentle nudge land on the same mark.
flick

Snaps from the projection

The pink dot is where the flick was headed. Throw it and it travels.
flick
§7

Rubber-banding — resist, don't stop

A hard clamp at a boundary reads as frozen — as though the app stopped listening. Progressive resistance says "still here, nothing more this way", which is a different sentence.

Hard clamp

Drag past the right edge. It sticks and your finger keeps going without it.
drag

Rubber-banded

Drag past the edge; it follows less and less, then springs back.
drag
Finding #2 in the review. The reader hard-clamps both zoom ([1, 5]) and pan. At either limit the page freezes under your fingers.
§7b

Spatial consistency — come from where you were summoned

A popover should grow out of the control that opened it. Scaling from its own centre severs the relationship between the button you pressed and the thing that appeared.

Grows from its own centre

Tap either button — the menu appears the same way regardless.

Grows from the trigger

The origin follows the button you actually pressed.
Finding #7 in the review. The app's .pick-pop sets a vertical origin only, so a menu from a right-hand toolbar button grows from the middle of itself.
§12

Materials — never stack glass on glass

Translucency conveys hierarchy, but two translucent layers over each other collapse into mud. The upper layer should be the heavier, more opaque one.

Glass over glass

Both layers blur what is behind. Read the second line.
Toolbar — 0.55 alpha, blur 14
Menu over it — 0.42 alpha, blur 14

Glass, then solid

The floating layer commits. Same background, same blur budget.
Toolbar — 0.55 alpha, blur 14
Menu over it — near-opaque, no blur
Finding #6 in the review. .pick-pop (0.78 + blur 26) opens over #reader-top (0.94 + blur 26). Also: at 0.94 that toolbar's own blur is paying GPU cost for almost nothing.
§15

Typography — tracking belongs to the size

Letters read further apart as they grow, so large text wants negative tracking and small text wants a touch of positive. One letter-spacing for everything is wrong somewhere.

One tracking everywhere

letter-spacing: 0.01em at every size.
Sarabande
Suite No. 1 in G major
Bar 17 ran nine cents sharp across four takes, and the tempo drifted two beats per minute over the repeat.

Tracking by size

-0.028em / -0.008em / +0.006em.
Sarabande
Suite No. 1 in G major
Bar 17 ran nine cents sharp across four takes, and the tempo drifted two beats per minute over the repeat.
Already right in the app. -0.02em at 3.3rem, -0.035em at 5.6rem, line-height: 1 on display figures against 1.5 on body.
§14

Reduced motion — gentler, not absent

The setting does not mean "no feedback". It means replace travel with a fade: the meaning still arrives, nothing crosses the eye to deliver it.

Motion removed entirely

Tap. It arrives with no acknowledgement at all — reads as a jump cut.
tap

Travel swapped for a fade

Tap. It still says something happened, without moving across the eye.
tap
Just fixed. Reduced motion is now answered once at the property level rather than a selector at a time, and prefers-reduced-transparency / prefers-contrast are answered at all — they were not before. Turn the settings on in System Settings and reload the app.
